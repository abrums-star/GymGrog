# Gym program research: fat loss, back focus, surf-flexible schedule

Researched 3 November 2026. Goal: lose weight (not get strong for its own sake), train neglected muscles (back / posterior chain), keep it fun with variation, and fit around surfing.

## 1. What the evidence actually says about training for fat loss

**Fat loss is driven mostly by the calorie deficit. Training's main job is to protect muscle, keep you moving, and keep you coming back.**

- A 2025 meta-analysis (36 studies, 1,564 people) found cardio-only and cardio+weights lost slightly more absolute fat mass than weights-only, but **percent body fat loss was the same across all modes**. Weights preserved about 0.9 kg more lean mass. Under 10 weeks, no difference at all. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40405489/) · [full text](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15502783.2025.2507949)
- A 2025 network meta-analysis of exercise *during a calorie deficit* found: aerobic work plus deficit ranked best for fat mass; **low/moderate-intensity resistance training beat high-intensity resistance training for fat loss during a deficit**; avoid deficits bigger than ~500 kcal/day to protect muscle. Sessions in the included trials were 30 to 60 min, 2 to 7 times a week. [Frontiers in Nutrition 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1579024/full)
- The European Association for the Study of Obesity guideline: moderate-intensity aerobic exercise for fat (including visceral fat) loss; moderate-to-high intensity resistance training to keep lean mass while losing weight. [EASO 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8365734/)
- A 2026 Frontiers in Endocrinology study found resistance training gave the largest fat-mass loss in their comparison and was the only mode that added lean mass. One study, treat as supportive not decisive. [Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2025.1725500/full)

**Practical translation**
1. Lift 2 to 3 times a week, moderate intensity, not grinding to failure every set. This is the "keep the muscle, burn energy, don't wreck yourself for surfing" zone.
2. Add 10 to 20 min of moderate cardio at the end of a lift, or on its own on non-surf, non-lift days. Surfing already counts as a big chunk of your aerobic work.
3. A modest food deficit (no more than ~500 kcal/day) and enough protein do more for the scale than any exercise choice. The app can track weight trend, not calories, unless you want it to.

## 2. Volume, reps, rest (hypertrophy and strength research, applied conservatively)

- Weekly sets per muscle group: 4 sets/week is the minimum that works; **5 to 10 sets/week per muscle is the efficient range**; more than 10 gives diminishing returns and more fatigue. (Pelland et al. 2025 meta-regression; 2022 umbrella review.) [umbrella review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9302196/)
- Load/reps: anything from 6 to 20+ reps builds muscle if the set is reasonably hard. For fat loss and joint comfort, **8 to 15 reps**, finishing each set with 2 to 3 reps still "in the tank" (RIR 2-3).
- Rest: **60 to 120 s** between sets is enough at this intensity. Shorter rests also keep heart rate up and the session under 50 min.
- Hitting each muscle **twice a week** is better than once at equal volume. Full-body sessions do this automatically.

## 3. Program structure for an irregular (surf-first) schedule

Full-body is the only structure that survives an unpredictable week: every session hits everything, so a missed session never leaves a body part untrained. Upper/lower only makes sense in weeks with 4 reliable gym days, which you won't have. [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/training/upper-lower-splits-explained) · [Kyle Hunt](https://kylehuntfitness.beehiiv.com/p/the-best-2-day-workout-splits-to-build-muscle-and-strength)

**Proposed: rotating full-body sessions, no calendar.** The app holds a queue of sessions (A, B, C) and always shows "next up". Whenever you go to the gym, you do the next one. The app also offers a "surfed today, short session" variant.

| Session | Emphasis | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A: Pull + legs | Back-heavy, hinge pattern | Your stated gap. Paddling muscles (lats, rear delts, erectors). |
| B: Push + legs | Chest/shoulders, squat pattern | Balance, pop-up strength. |
| C: Full body circuit | Lighter, higher reps, shorter rests, cardio finish | Fat-loss day, easy on a tired body. |

Each session: ~45 min. 5 to 6 exercises, 3 sets each, 10 to 15 reps, 60 to 90 s rest, 10 min cardio finish (optional, skip when you surfed).

Regularity target the app tracks: **2 sessions a week minimum, 3 is the goal**, measured as a rolling 14-day count rather than fixed days. Surf days count as "active" for streak purposes.

## 4. Back / posterior chain for a surfer

Paddling is pull-dominant and lives in the whole posterior chain: lats, rear delts, erector spinae, plus triceps. Upper-body pulling strength (pull-ups, rows) measurably improved paddling endurance in one controlled study. Evidence base is thin and mostly surf-industry articles, but the exercise selection is uncontroversial. [ECU study](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/2555) · [Rapture Camps](https://www.rapturecamps.com/learn-to-surf/surf-fitness/paddle-strength-training)

Candidate movements (vary 2 to 3 of these per cycle):
- Horizontal pull: seated cable row, chest-supported machine row, single-arm dumbbell row, TRX row
- Vertical pull: lat pulldown (wide, neutral, close grip), assisted pull-up
- Rear delts / upper back: face pulls, reverse pec-deck, band pull-aparts
- Hinge: Romanian deadlift (dumbbell or bar), kettlebell swing, back extension bench, single-leg RDL
- Posterior core: bird-dog, "superman" / prone cobra, cable pull-through

## 5. Variation: how much and why

Research says rotating exercises improves **motivation and adherence** but does not give more muscle than a fixed program when volume is equal (Baz-Valle 2019, PLoS ONE). Changing every 4 to 6 weeks is coaching convention, not a tested threshold. [PMC6934277](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934277)

**App rule:** keep the *pattern* fixed (horizontal pull, vertical pull, hinge, squat, push, carry/core) and swap the *exercise* within the pattern every 4-week block, from a pool of exercises you have rated "like / ok / never". Weight history is kept per exercise, so when a swap comes back around the app knows your last working weight.
